Why property tax is listed separately — and isn't double-counted.

You're right that a mortgage usually bundles taxes into the monthly payment: the lender collects 1/12 of the $6,130 tax (plus insurance) each month into an escrow account and pays the county for you. So your real monthly check to the bank is PITI — Principal + Interest + Taxes + Insurance — all in one. The loan you actually borrow and pay interest on is just the P&I part; the escrow is simply the mechanism that passes your tax money through.

That's why the same tax shows up as $6,130/yr here (the annual, unlevered cap-rate view) and as ~$511/mo in the financed PITI card above — the $511 line is that escrowed tax. It is the same cost shown two ways, not two separate costs, and it is never added on top of the loan.

Separately, mortgage interest and property tax are deductible on your income taxes — but that's an after-the-fact reduction of your year-end tax bill, not a reduction of the monthly cash you pay to own and operate the property. Cap rate and cash flow are measured on actual cash in and out before income-tax effects, which is why the full tax stays in as an operating cost.
